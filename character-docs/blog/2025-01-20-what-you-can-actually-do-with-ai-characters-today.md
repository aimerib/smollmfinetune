## What Makes These Characters Different

### They Have Actual Personalities

Most AI chatbots are helpful assistants. Our characters are **people** (digital people, but still). They have:

**Consistent Psychological Profiles**: Built on the Big Five personality model, characters respond predictably based on their traits. A highly neurotic character will consistently react with anxiety to stress, while a character high in openness will be curious about new ideas.

**Emotional Persistence**: Characters remember how conversations made them feel and carry those emotions forward. Make a character angry, and they'll still be a little cool toward you next time you talk.

**Relationship Dynamics**: Characters form opinions about each other. They can become friends, rivals, or anything in between based on their interactions.

### They Remember Everything

Unlike chatbots that start fresh each time, our characters build persistent memories:

**Conversation History**: Characters remember what you've talked about, creating continuity across conversations
**Formed Relationships**: They remember how they feel about other characters and people
**World Knowledge**: Characters learn facts about their world and can reference them naturally
**Personal Growth**: Characters can change and develop based on their experiences

### They Exist in Rich Worlds

Characters aren't floating in a void. They come from somewhere:

**Detailed Lore**: Every character exists in a world with history, geography, cultures, and conflicts
**Consistent World Rules**: Characters all share knowledge about their world and respect its rules
**Interconnected Stories**: Characters can reference events, places, and people from their shared world
**Dynamic Environments**: Worlds can evolve and change, affecting all characters within them

## Console-Quality Conversations (New!)

Our latest breakthrough brings **spatial audio** to AI character conversations. This isn't just a gimmick - it fundamentally changes how interactions feel.

### What Spatial Audio Means

Put on headphones and start a conversation with multiple characters. You'll hear:
- **Alice speaking from your left** while **Bob responds from your right**
- **Realistic environmental acoustics** - conversations in a tavern sound different from those in a cathedral
- **Natural interruptions** - characters can cut each other off just like real people
- **Distance effects** - characters farther away sound appropriately distant

### Real-World Example: The Tavern Scene

Imagine you're developing a fantasy RPG. You create three characters:
- **Elena** (wise mage): Positioned left, thoughtful and measured
- **Marcus** (bold warrior): Center front, loud and confident  
- **Finn** (clever rogue): Right back, sarcastic and quick-witted

Start a conversation about a quest. Elena begins explaining the magical theory behind an ancient curse. Marcus interrupts with impatience, wanting to know about the combat challenges. Finn interjects from the side with a dry comment about how they're both missing the real danger.

The conversation feels **spatially real**. You can close your eyes and feel like you're actually sitting at a table with these three people.

## Current Limitations (Let's Be Honest)

### What We're Still Working On

**Visual Representation**: Characters currently exist as voice and text only. Visual avatars are on our roadmap but not available yet.

**Physical World Interaction**: Characters can discuss objects and environments, but can't directly manipulate virtual objects.

**Model Training**: While characters have consistent personalities, they can't learn new skills or drastically change their core traits through conversation alone.

**Language Support**: Currently optimized for English, though characters can speak other languages their base models support.

### What We Won't Promise (Yet)

**General AI**: These are sophisticated character systems, not general artificial intelligence.

**Perfect Human Mimicry**: Characters feel believable but don't pretend to be actual humans.

**Real-Time Learning**: Characters form memories and relationships but don't fundamentally retrain based on conversations.

**Unlimited Context**: While characters have good memories, extremely long conversation histories may need summarization.
